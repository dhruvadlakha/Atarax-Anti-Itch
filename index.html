Why does itch due to atopic dermatitis often seem much worse after bedtime?1-3

The same child, two stories

Manageable in clinic. Miserable after bedtime.

Why does a child with itch due to atopic dermatitis seem manageable in your clinic — yet miserable once the lights go out at home? The daytime examination and the night-time reality can tell two very different stories.

In clinic

Under bright clinic light, the skin can look settled and the child appears comfortable.

At night

At home after dark, the same child may scratch, wake and struggle to settle.

The real burden may begin after lights out.

After lights out

The story often changes once the room goes dark.

He scratches most at night.

Bedtime has become stressful.

He wakes up repeatedly.

Nobody sleeps properly.

Why night-time symptoms matter

Night-time itch may reflect more than visible skin findings.

The examination captures a moment; the night captures the burden. These are the consequences that rarely show up in a daytime consultation.

Sleep disruption

Poor sleep affects recovery and daytime function.2,3

Behavioural impact

Irritability, poor concentration and mood changes.2,3

Family burden

Impacts parents, siblings and overall family well-being.3

Under-recognized severity

Skin may look better in clinic, but the night-time burden tells the real story.2

Decreased quality of life

For child and caregiver — affecting daily activities, school performance and emotional well-being.2,3

Risk of secondary skin infection

Intense scratching can break the skin barrier, increasing infection risk.1,2

Day exam → night burden
